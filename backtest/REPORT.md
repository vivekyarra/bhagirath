# Backtest report — would Bhagirath have warned in time?

**Short answer.** For Indore's Bhagirathpura, yes. The complaints that residents had already made, left open without a verified fix, would have triggered a **Watch alert 23 to 653 days before the first reported death**, depending on how strictly the evidence is read. With nobody responding, the escalation ladder would then have put an evidence pack on the commissioner's desk within six days. The stronger, multi-signal alerts (Warning and Critical) fire only once illness and test data appear, which is when the outbreak was already under way. For the three other outbreaks, the available public data would **not** have produced an early warning, and this report says why.

Every number below comes from [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md). That file is generated by `bhagirath backtest` from the data in [`data/`](data/) and the frozen rule, and CI regenerates it on every commit and fails if it differs.

Examples: the "300 m" condition is pairwise, unlocated complaints never cluster, anonymous complaints count as one household, and a patient listed under both diarrhoea and vomiting counts once.

## 2. Method

1. **Sources.** Only public reporting was used: news, government statements, the CAG report, the Rajya Sabha answer, and published outbreak investigations. Each CSV row has its `source_url`, the outlet's publication date, a verbatim quote of 30 words or less, and notes. [`RESEARCH_NOTES.md`](RESEARCH_NOTES.md) records the search, every URL opened (including failures), conflicts between sources, and what could not be found. Names of private individuals are withheld.
2. **Scope.** Every row is marked `in_ward` = yes or no. City-wide, zone-wide and other-ward rows are kept for context but excluded from the replay. Examples: the 400 city-wide 311 complaints, the CAG's 2013–18 city samples, and a Ward 24 test.
3. **Placement in time** (spec §9.1 step 3). A row with a date range is replayed in two scenarios:
   - **best**: placed from its earliest plausible date, with counts spread evenly across the range;
   - **conservative**: placed at its latest plausible date. Undated rows go to the date they were published.
4. **Inputs the rule can see.** None of the historical complaints has a geocoded location, so the 300 m cluster condition can never fire. Only "a complaint open more than 7 days without a verified fix" can. Illness rows count only diarrhoea/vomiting cases, so jaundice and typhoid counts are not inputs.
5. **Replay.** The unchanged production function `rule.Evaluate` runs every 6 hours, and at every event, across each timeline, with no look-ahead.
6. **Escalation timing.** This assumes no official acknowledges anything, following the ladder in `config/ladder.example.yaml`: Watch goes to rung 1 and then times out after 72 h to rung 2 and after another 72 h to rung 3; Warning goes to rungs 1, 2 and the health officer, with rung 3 after 48 h; Critical goes to every rung at once.
7. **Sensitivity** ([`sensitivity.yaml`](sensitivity.yaml)). Pre-declared variants can only remove evidence, never add or move it.

## 3. Results

### Indore — Bhagirathpura (Ward 11), Dec 2025 – Feb 2026

First death: 21 Dec 2025 as claimed (cremation records), or 29 Dec 2025 as corroborated (named in several outlets). First hospitalisation: 25 Dec 2025 (one family's account), or 29 Dec 2025 officially. Deaths: official tolls rose from 3–7 (31 Dec) to 16 (state death audit, 28 Jan); the judicial commission counted 22 "possibly related"; residents claim 36.

| Evidence used | Scenario | First Watch | Watch before first death (claimed / corroborated) | Commissioner has evidence pack by | First Warning or higher |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All rows about Bhagirathpura | best / conservative | 7 Mar 2024 | 653 / 661 days | 13 Mar 2024 | 24 Dec 2025 / 29 Dec 2025 |
| Without the Feb 2024 councillor letter | best | 11 May 2025 | 223 / 231 days | 17 May 2025 | 24 Dec 2025 |
| Without the Feb 2024 councillor letter | conservative | 27 Nov 2025 | 23 / 31 days | 3 Dec 2025 | 29 Dec 2025 |
| Only the 3 individually dated 2025 helpline complaints | best | 22 Oct 2025 | 59 / 67 days | 28 Oct 2025 | 24 Dec 2025 |
| Only the 3 individually dated 2025 helpline complaints | conservative | 27 Nov 2025 | **23 / 31 days** | **3 Dec 2025** | 29 Dec 2025 |

**Reading this honestly.**

- The headline figure we stand behind is the **most conservative row**. It uses only the three helpline complaints that a news report dates individually, each placed at its latest plausible date. Even then, the frozen rule raises a Watch on **27 Nov 2025**, and an unanswered ladder reaches the commissioner by **3 Dec 2025**. That is three to four weeks before people started dying.
- The early warning comes from **one signal only**: complaints left open without a verified fix. Without Bhagirath, those complaints were marked "resolved" or sat "under resolution". Under Bhagirath, an official's "fixed" counts only after the resident confirms it, so the complaints would have stayed open and climbed the ladder. That accountability loop is the product. It does not depend on predicting anything.
- The **multi-signal** alerts (Warning on illness or a failed test, Critical with five or more cases) come only on **24–30 Dec 2025**. That is the same week as the first deaths. With the public data available, Bhagirath would not have issued a two-signal warning before the outbreak. No dated, ward-specific water test exists before 29 Dec 2025.
- The 653-day figure rests on a single councillor's letter from Feb 2024, which may concern an earlier, separate problem. We report it, but we do not lead with it.

### Shimla — hepatitis E/jaundice, Dec 2015 – May 2016

| Scenario | First Watch | First Warning or higher | First Critical |
|---|---|---|---|
| best | 9 Jan 2016 | 18 Jan 2016 | never |
| conservative | 9 Jan 2016 | 31 Jan 2016 | never |

The illness began in the second week of December 2015, so this is **not an early warning**. The two complaint rows are undated, so they can only be placed at their publication dates. The illness was jaundice, which is not a diarrhoea/vomiting input. Coliform testing started only on 18 Jan 2016. The first death's exact date is unknown, but one death had been reported by 20 Jan 2016. **What would have been needed:** dated resident complaints about turbid water, or any routine bacteriological test of the Ashwani Khud supply in December.

### Gandhinagar — typhoid, late Dec 2025 – Jan 2026

First Watch on **3 Jan 2026** in both scenarios, from a post-outbreak test; no Warning. More than 100 children were already in hospital by then, so this is **not an early warning**. The only pre-outbreak complaint evidence is one outlet saying residents complained "days before", with no date and no count. Typhoid is a fever illness, so clinic diarrhoea counts would not capture it. **What would have been needed:** dated complaints about the new pipeline's foul-smelling water, which Bhagirath's missed-call and WhatsApp intake are built to capture, and a typhoid-aware illness signal. The latter would mean a new rule version; it is not a change to v1.

### Greater Noida — Delta 1 sector, Jan 2026 (control)

First Watch on **7 Jan 2026**, from 7 diarrhoea/vomiting cases at a health camp; no Warning. The authority's samples came back clean, and no one was hospitalised or died. The rule raises a single-signal Watch and goes no further, which is the right response to a small episode with a negative test.

## 4. Limitations

- **The data comes from news reports.** Dates are often relative ("six months", "mid-November") and were bracketed mechanically, with the bracket recorded in each row's notes. One key Indore complaint has a conflicting date (15 Oct, or 15 Dec in a Hindi rework). The replay uses the whole 15 Oct–15 Dec range, so the conservative scenario takes 15 Dec.
- **Secondary sources.** The three dated Indore complaints come from an outlet reworking an Indian Express report that we could not open. The judicial commission report and the High Court orders could not be loaded, so we relied on press summaries.
- **No locations.** No historical complaint is geocoded, so the cluster condition — the rule's main complaint signal in a live pilot — is untested by this backtest. In a pilot, locations come from GPS pins, landmarks and registered addresses.
- **Cumulative counts.** Illness sources mostly report cumulative totals, and overlapping rows can double-count. This cannot move a threshold crossing earlier than the first row that crosses on its own, but it does inflate the case numbers quoted in the reason text.
- **Assumed participation.** The counterfactual assumes residents who complained to helplines would also have reached Bhagirath, by missed call, WhatsApp or a volunteer. That assumption is what the pilot tests.
- **The ladder timing assumes silence.** Real officials might acknowledge early. That would be good, but acknowledgement alone does not close a complaint: a fix needs evidence and the resident's confirmation.
- **Four outbreaks are a small sample.** Two fit the rule's inputs poorly (jaundice, typhoid). We report them anyway; the spec requires that we show where the rule would not have fired.

## 5. Reproduce

```bash
go run ./cmd/bhagirath backtest        # rewrites backtest/RESULTS.md and results.json
go test ./internal/backtest/           # fails if RESULTS.md differs from a fresh run
```
